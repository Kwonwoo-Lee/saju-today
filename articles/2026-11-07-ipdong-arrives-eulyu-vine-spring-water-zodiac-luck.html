November 7: Ipdong Lands for Real at 6:52 PM on an Eulyu Day Where the Vine Finally Goes Still Over Spring Water - 3 Zodiac Animals With the Best Luck

Published November 7, 2026

November 7, 2026 is the day winter actually begins on Korea's traditional calendar - Ipdong (입동), the solar term marking winter's first day, lands for real this evening at 6:52 PM, not just as an eve anymore. Today runs on an Eulyu (乙酉) day: Yin Wood - the vine and blossom kind, not a standing tree - sitting directly on a Rooster branch of pure Metal, the same Metal-over-Wood clash that cut through yesterday's Gapsin day, now finishing what it started. The day's classical nayin name hasn't changed either: it's still Cheonjungsu, 泉中水, 'spring water' - except tonight, for the first time, that hidden water finally steps into its own season, since winter itself runs on Water in the five-element calendar. Three zodiac animals are caught right at that hinge.

1. Rooster - Standing Exactly on the Hinge

It's the Rooster's own branch holding up today, the one day in sixty where the hour of Ipdong itself and the Rooster's branch land together. There's no need to brace for a change that's still coming - the change is happening right now, this evening, while you're standing in it. Whatever you've been waiting to start until "winter proper" arrives, that moment is tonight, not some day down the road, and it rewards being decisive rather than watching the clock.

2. Dragon - Where Metal Finally Sets

Dragon is the one branch that locks directly with Rooster's: 辰酉, a Six Harmony pair that fuses into solid Metal rather than clashing with it. Something you and someone else have only loosely agreed on - a deal still running on a handshake - firms up cleanly today if you're willing to put it in writing. Metal that's been forming all week finally sets into its finished shape tonight, and what gets sealed now holds.

3. Rabbit - Feeling the Hinge Swing Hardest

Rabbit sits exactly opposite Rooster on the wheel: 卯酉, the one direct clash point across from today's branch, so the turn from autumn into winter lands on Rabbit harder than on anyone else today. That's not bad luck - it's the hour of change finding the animal built to feel it first. Something that's been quietly ending anyway is better let go tonight than held onto past its season; the sooner it turns, the sooner the next thing can start.
