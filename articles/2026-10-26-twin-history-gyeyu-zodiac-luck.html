October 26: Two Turning Points in Korean History Meet a Gyeyu Metal-Water Day - 3 Zodiac Signs With the Best Luck

Published October 26, 2026

October 26 carries unusual weight in Korean history. On this day in 1909, independence activist Ahn Jung-geun shot and killed Ito Hirobumi, Japan's resident-general in Korea, at Harbin railway station in Manchuria - 2026 marks 117 years since. Exactly seventy years later to the date, in 1979, what's known in Korea as the October 26 Incident ended President Park Chung-hee's eighteen-year rule in a single night; this year marks 47 years since that event. Korean news outlets still note each October that the calendar quietly repeats itself this way. On the Saju side, today falls on Gyeyu (癸酉): Yin Water sitting directly on the Rooster's Metal branch. Unlike a day where metal stays hidden underneath a branch, here the support is already out in the open - Metal openly feeding the Water above it. It suits decisions that no longer need to stay concealed. Today's branch also anchors one of Saju's three-way alliances, the Snake-Rooster-Ox Metal Trine, and three zodiac animals are riding that current hardest.

1. Rooster - Standing in Plain Sight

Today's branch is the Rooster's own sign, so the usual instinct to double-check and hedge can ease off a little today. What you've already built is solid enough to show as-is. A plan you've been quietly polishing is ready to go out under your own name rather than staying in draft form.

2. Ox - The Trine's Steady Anchor

The Ox completes the Snake-Rooster-Ox Metal Trine from its most grounded point, and that steadiness pays off today - a long-running arrangement finally gets its formal paperwork, or patient groundwork laid weeks ago quietly becomes official. Nothing dramatic; just a door that was ajar finally closing properly.

3. Snake - Where the Turn Happens

As the third point of today's Metal trine, the Snake carries the day's turning-point energy most directly. A conversation that's circled for too long can resolve in one clean exchange, and a chapter that's been dragging is more likely to close in a single motion than drift on. Say the thing plainly; today rewards it.
