October 3: The 3 Zodiac Animals With the Best Luck on Gaecheonjeol, Korea's 4,359th Founding Day

Published October 3, 2026

Today is Gaecheonjeol, Korea's National Foundation Day, which marks the legendary founding of the ancient kingdom of Gojoseon by Dangun in 2333 BCE — making this the 4,359th anniversary. It falls on a Saturday this year, but a substitute holiday on Monday, October 5 still stretches it into a full three-day weekend. The founding myth is also a patience story: a bear and a tiger both asked to become human, and only the bear endured 100 days in a cave eating nothing but mugwort and garlic to succeed. That same quiet persistence is lighting up a few zodiac animals today.

1. Ox — The Bear's Patience, Paid Off

If any sign channels the bear of the founding myth, it's the Ox. Something you've been grinding at quietly for weeks — with no real sign it was working — starts showing results right around now. Resist the urge to switch strategy just because progress feels slow; today rewards staying the course rather than restarting.

2. Rabbit — A Clean Start Lands Well

Founding days are made for new beginnings, and the Rabbit's instinct for careful timing is unusually sharp today. A plan you've been hesitant to commit to — a small purchase, a new routine, a first step on something — goes more smoothly if you start it over this long weekend instead of waiting for a "better" moment. Caution has served you well, but today it's safe to move.

3. Rooster — Discipline Gets Noticed

The Rooster's habit of showing up prepared and on schedule stands out today, especially against the relaxed pace of a holiday weekend. Someone is paying more attention than usual to who follows through and who doesn't, so a commitment kept today — even a small one — builds real credibility that outlasts the long weekend.

Not One of Today's Three? That's Fine.

Missing out on today's especially lucky energy doesn't mean the holiday won't treat you well. These three animals simply have the clearest tailwind on this particular Gaecheonjeol — most people will still enjoy a well-earned day off either way.
