November 2: All Souls' Day Meets a Gyeongjin Day's White Wax Metal - 3 Zodiac Animals With the Best Luck

Published November 2, 2026

November 2 is All Souls' Day across the Catholic world, the day set aside to pray for and remember the dead. It opens what Korea's Catholic Church calls 위령성월, the whole Month of Souls, and cathedrals and cemeteries across the country fill with people lighting candles for family who've passed. On the Saju calendar, today is a Gyeongjin (庚辰) day: Yang Metal sitting on an Earth Dragon that the old readings treat as a vault where water gathers and keeps. The classical name for this stem-branch pair is Baeknap-geum, 白蠟金 - White Wax Metal, literally the metal once cast for candlesticks and wax seals, an odd little echo for a day built around lit candles. It closes out the Monkey-Rat-Dragon Water Trine, and three zodiac animals are carrying that current today.

1. Dragon - The Vault That Holds It

It's the Dragon's own branch under today's sky, and the Dragon's job in this trine isn't to rush - it's to hold. It's the vault where the Water Trine's current finally settles and keeps. Whatever you've been carrying forward for someone else - a promise, a debt of gratitude, an old photo you keep meaning to frame - deserves ten quiet minutes today. You're not behind; you're the one holding the memory steady for everyone else.

2. Monkey - Where the Spring First Broke Ground

The Monkey opens the Water Trine, the spot where this current first broke through the ground. An old contact, an old file, or a half-finished idea from months back resurfaces today looking for a reason to start moving again. Answer the message or reopen the folder - what looks like an interruption is actually the water finding its way back to you.

3. Rat - The Current at Full Flood

The Rat sits at the Water Trine's peak, where the current runs fullest and fastest. Today rewards speed and plain talk over caution - a negotiation, a pitch, or a hard conversation that's been dragging moves faster than you planned, and that's good news. Say the clear version of what you mean; the water's already moving your way, and slow, careful phrasing will only dam it up.
